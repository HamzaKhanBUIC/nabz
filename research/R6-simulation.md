# R6: Simulation design for sample crew data

## Short answer

- **Signals (baseline, spread, circadian, months 3–6 drift):** Healthy astronaut-relevant baselines and ISS spaceflight shifts are well anchored for ambulatory blood pressure (preflight 129/85 mmHg dropping by −8/−9 mmHg over 3–6 months on ISS with preserved ~8 mmHg nocturnal dip), resting heart rate (healthy adult mean 65.5 ± 7.7 bpm; 24-h HR unchanged over 3–6 months in space), sleep duration (NASA schedules 8.5 h; ISS crews average 6.09 ± 0.67 h/night), body mass (−2.8% SLAMMD to −4.4% BMMD in the first 30 days, then plateau through 6 months), and deep-space GCR radiation (1.84 ± 0.33 mSv/day during Mars transit). PVT-B (3-min Reaction Self-Test) uses a ≥355 ms lapse threshold (rested baseline ~230–290 ms, near-zero lapses) and degrades with <6 h sleep, while subjective VAS mood/stress/fatigue scales (0–100) show wide inter-individual differences across 520-day confinement.
- **Events to inject:** Five realistic anomaly shapes are supported by peer-reviewed effect sizes: (1) a 7-day short-sleep block (4–6 h/night drives linear day-by-day PVT lapse accumulation while subjective sleepiness plateaus early); (2) viral/respiratory illness (median +7 bpm RHR rise starting ~4 days pre-symptom onset; ISS immune clinical incidence 3.40 events/flight-year); (3) a solar particle event (hours-long rise, ~12 h peak at 2–3× background on Mars surface or ~24.7 mSv cumulative across 5 cruise SPEs, ~36 h decay, 1–2 day shelter); (4) high-workload/slam-shift stress before a key task (9–12 h sleep inversion, EVA exertion up to 85% VO2 peak, Mission Control conflict spikes); and (5) cabin CO2 rise (headache odds double per +1 mmHg CO2; NASA-STD-3001 caps 1-h cabin ppCO2 at ≤3.0 mmHg; cognitive results are mixed between Satish 2012 office drops and Scully 2019 astronaut-like subjects).
- **Calibration datasets:** Direct empirical checks are available from PhysioNet (`mmash`, `dreamt`, `sleep-edfx`, `wearable-exam-stress`, `big-ideas-glycemic-wearable`), OpenNeuro (`ds000201` Stockholm Sleepy Brain Study with 5-min PVT-192), NSRR (`sleepdata.org`), and NASA OSDR (`OSD-570` to `OSD-687` for Inspiration4 multi-omics/clinical, noting raw Apple Watch and Cognition app files are access-controlled via SOMA/ALSDA).
- **Simulation method & open-source generators:** Standard physiological time-series synthesis combines four additive/state-space layers: individual baseline + slow mission-drift curve (e.g., 30-day step/plateau for mass and BP), 24-h circadian cosinor, autocorrelated mean-reverting noise (AR(1) or Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process), and parameterized event kernels (boxcar/linear ramp for sleep debt, pre-onset rise + decay for illness, biexponential impulse for SPEs/stress). Worthwhile open-source Python tools are `TimeSynth` (harmonic + Ornstein–Uhlenbeck/AR(p) + noise) and `NeuroKit2` (`nk.signal_simulate`, `nk.ecg_simulate`).
- **Key gaps:** Several exact daily dispersion numbers (intraindividual day-to-day SD for ISS PVT-B mean RT, VAS mood/stress/fatigue SDs, exact Mars-transit daily GCR SD separated from instrument uncertainty, and exact ISS slam-shift BP/RHR deltas) could not be extracted from open abstracts/summaries and are listed under *Not verified / open questions*.

## What this means for the app (proposal)

- **Proposal — Crew-of-4 baseline profiles:** Initialize 4 distinct crew personas around astronaut-age healthy norms anchored to the sourced means and between-person spreads above (e.g., individual RHR baselines drawn from ~55–72 bpm per Quer et al. 2020; preflight SBP/DBP ~115–130 / 75–85 mmHg; rested PVT-B mean RT ~240–275 ms with 0–1 baseline lapses; baseline body mass ~65–85 kg; preflight sleep ~6.5–7.5 h).
- **Proposal — Months 1–6 spaceflight drift curves (up to & past Mission Day 120):**
  - *SBP / DBP:* Apply a smooth exponential adaptation over days 1–30 settling at −8 mmHg SBP and −9 mmHg DBP relative to Earth preflight (Norsk et al. 2015), holding steady through Day 120–180 while preserving a circadian dip if sampled multiple times per day.
  - *Body mass:* Apply a −2.8% to −4.4% ramp over days 1–30 followed by a flat plateau through Day 120+ (Zwart et al. 2014).
  - *Sleep duration:* Shift from ~6.5 h preflight to an in-flight mean ~6.1 h/night with day-to-day SD ~0.67 h (Barger et al. 2014).
  - *Resting heart rate:* Keep 24-h baseline flat relative to preflight (Norsk et al. 2015).
  - *Daily radiation dose:* Generate continuous GCR background centered at 1.84 mSv/day (Zeitlin et al. 2013), accumulating ~221 mSv by Day 120 before SPEs.
  - *Mood / stress / fatigue (0–100 VAS):* Give 2–3 crew members flat resilient baselines and 1–2 crew members mild upward cumulative drift across mission quarters (Basner et al. 2014).
- **Proposal — Autocorrelated daily generator (`AR(1)` / Ornstein–Uhlenbeck + circadian + events):** Generate each crew member's daily signal `y_i(t) = μ_i + drift_i(t) + circadian_i(hour) + x_i(t) + Σ events_i(t)`, where `x_i(t) = φ * x_i(t−1) + ε_t` (with `φ ≈ 0.4–0.7` so multi-day runs look realistic rather than i.i.d. white noise) implemented in Python using `TimeSynth` or a lightweight NumPy/SciPy state-space script.
- **Proposal — Injected event shapes for testing the Action Card & Normal Zone:**
  1. *Short-sleep week (Days 35–41):* Drop sleep to 4.5–5.5 h/night; apply a linear day-by-day ramp in PVT-B mean RT and lapses (peaking on Day 7) and elevate VAS stress/fatigue, while plateauing subjective sleepiness early (Van Dongen et al. 2003; Jones et al. 2022).
  2. *Viral illness (e.g., Days 62–70):* Inject a +5 to +9 bpm RHR rise (centered on +7 bpm median) starting 3–4 days before peak fatigue/VAS symptom onset and decaying over 4–5 days (Mishra et al. 2020).
  3. *Solar Particle Event (e.g., Day 88–90):* Inject a sharp multi-hour rise, ~12 h plateau, and ~36 h exponential decay adding ~5–25 mSv depending on shelter compliance (Zeitlin 2013; Ehresmann 2018), paired with a temporary sleep/stress bump from storm-shelter confinement.
  4. *High-workload / slam-shift task (e.g., Days 105–108 before a Day 108 inspection/EVA):* Invert sleep window (9–12 h shift) causing 1–2 nights <5 h sleep, elevated VAS workload/stress, and acute PVT slowing.
  5. *Cabin CO2 rise (e.g., Days 116–119 leading into Day 120):* Ramp cabin ppCO2 from ~2.0 mmHg up to ~3.5–4.5 mmHg (above NASA-STD-3001's 3.0 mmHg 1-h cap), triggering headache/fatigue VAS flags and mild cognitive slowing so Sara sees a concrete multi-signal deviation by Day 120.
- **Proposal — Calibration check:** Before locking generator parameters, run summary distribution checks (mean, day-to-day SD, lag-1 autocorrelation, and tail quantiles) against PhysioNet `mmash` / `wearable-exam-stress` (for HR/stress spread) and OpenNeuro `ds000201` (for rested vs 3-h sleep-restricted PVT RT distributions).

## Not verified / open questions

- **Exact day-to-day intraindividual SDs missing from open abstracts:** We could not verify peer-reviewed day-to-day within-person SDs in healthy astronaut-age adults (specifically on-orbit or daily morning check-ins) for (a) 3-min PVT-B mean RT (ms) and lapse counts, (b) VAS mood/stress/fatigue scores (0–100) in Jones et al. 2022 / Basner et al. 2014, (c) absolute day-to-day SBP/DBP SD in mmHg (Juhanoja 2017 reports outcome CV thresholds >11.0% / >12.8%, not healthy-astronaut SD in mmHg), and (d) true day-to-day physical variability (SD) of deep-space GCR mSv/day separate from the ±0.33 mSv/day measurement uncertainty in Zeitlin et al. (2013).
- **Exact time-of-day (circadian) amplitudes in ms and VAS points:** While the circadian phase pattern is established, exact verified peak-to-trough amplitudes (in ms for PVT-B and in points for VAS mood/stress/fatigue) and exact ISS circadian BP/RHR cosinor amplitudes (in mmHg/bpm) could not be traced to an open primary table during this session and are left unquantified here rather than interpolated.
- **Exact physiological effect sizes for ISS slam shifts and acute viral URI on PVT/sleep:** Quantitative deltas (exact mmHg BP change, exact sleep-hour loss, and exact PVT ms slowing) specifically measured during ISS slam shifts or acute upper respiratory infections on orbit were not confirmed in accessible open-text results; Mishra et al. (2020) gives the verified +7 bpm RHR median for viral infection, and Van Dongen (2003) / Jones (2022) cover sleep-loss effects.
- **CO2 cognitive dose-response discrepancy:** Satish et al. (2012) and Allen et al. (2016) report substantial cognitive score drops at 950–2,500 ppm, whereas Scully et al. (2019) in astronaut-like subjects found a dip at 1,200 ppm but recovery at 2,500–5,000 ppm. Whether our Mars transit simulation should inject a cognitive/PVT penalty during a CO2 rise—or only headache/fatigue symptoms per Law et al. (2014)—remains an open modeling choice.
- **OSDR raw wearable/PVT file availability:** While NASA OSDR lists Inspiration4 studies (`OSD-570` to `OSD-687`), raw Apple Watch telemetry and Cognition app scores are governed by restricted-access SOMA/ALSDA protocols, so direct automated calibration will need to rely primarily on PhysioNet, OpenNeuro (`ds000201`), and NSRR (`sleepdata.org`) unless restricted OSDR access is requested.
