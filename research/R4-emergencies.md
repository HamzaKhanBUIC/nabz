# R4: First-person emergency mode

## Short answer
- **What happens in flight:** NASA's Human Research Program scores in-flight medical conditions as a "red" (highest likelihood, highest consequence) risk for Mars because missions are Earth-independent with up to ~22-minute one-way communication delays and no evacuation. On the ISS, high-frequency events are skin rashes (1.12/flight year), prolonged nasal congestion/rhinitis (1.0/flight year), and minor hand abrasions/lacerations (0.021/flight day men, 0.015/flight day women), whereas high-severity risk drivers in NASA's Integrated Medical Model (IMM) include ocular chemical burns, renal stones, acute cardiac events, severe trauma, and sepsis.
- **Candidates (solo manageability):** A lone user can realistically self-manage **bleeding cuts** (minor cuts and conscious extremity pressure/tourniquet), **minor burns**, **mild allergic rashes or initial epinephrine auto-injection**, **eye debris/chemical flushing**, and **conscious presyncope (near-fainting)**—provided red flags immediately trigger crewmate/CMO help and an Earth Flight Surgeon alert. **Chest pain**, **thunderclap/neurological headaches**, **toxic air/smoke inhalation**, and **complicated kidney stones** quickly exceed solo self-care and serve primarily as rapid triage and escalation triggers.
- **MVP recommendation (proposal):** Include **4 emergencies**—**Bleeding cut**, **Burn**, **Eye injury / sudden vision change**, and **Allergic reaction** (with **Dizziness / presyncope** as an optional 5th linked to BP check-in)—because they combine high spaceflight incidence or consequence with immediate, time-critical first-person steps that cannot wait 40 minutes for an Earth reply.
- **Safety wording:** Follow patterns from NHS 111, Red Cross, Ada, and FDA CDS principles: state clearly that the guide supports emergency first-aid decisions and is **not a medical diagnosis**, surface red-flag escalation before/during steps, and provide a one-tap crew/Earth alert whenever symptoms worsen or cross clinical thresholds.

## What this means for the app (proposal)
- **Proposed MVP Emergency Set (4 core procedures + 1 optional link):**
  1. **Bleeding cut** — Highest routine trauma probability in orbit (hands/exercise/hardware); unambiguous one-person action sequence (pressure vs wash vs tourniquet).
  2. **Burn** — High first-person impact; prevents harmful myths (ice/butter) and gives an on-screen 5–20 min timer for cool-water rinsing.
  3. **Eye injury or sudden vision change** — High evacuation driver in Mars IMM modeling (chemical burn/debris); immediate self-flush cannot wait for Earth's 40-minute round-trip delay, while sudden vision loss triggers instant escalation.
  4. **Allergic reaction** — Skin/hypersensitivity rashes are the #1 ISS clinical symptom, and two-branch triage safely separates mild rash self-care from immediate self-injected epinephrine + crew alert.
  5. *(Optional 5th)* **Dizziness / near-fainting** — Connects naturally to Sara's guided blood-pressure check-in using 2024 AHA/ARC lower-body counterpressure steps while conscious.
- **Why defer the other 4 from MVP:** **Chest pain** and **severe thunderclap headache** demand crewmate/CMO and Earth evaluation almost immediately with little safe solo treatment; **smoke or toxic air** is driven by spacecraft Caution & Warning hardware procedures rather than a personal health app; **kidney stone pain** requires onboard ultrasound (AMOS) and prescription/IV care once obstruction or fever appears. (If shown in UI, keep them as 1-screen red-flag escalation cards.)
- **Proposed short screen instructions (offline, ≥12 pt, max ~12 words/step, persistent red-flag alert button):**
  - **Bleeding cut:**
    1. *Severe bleed?* Press firm, steady with clean cloth. Do not peek early.
    2. *Soaked through?* Add another cloth on top. Keep pressing hard.
    3. *Still pouring on arm/leg?* Place tourniquet 2 in above wound (never on joint).
    4. *Minor cut?* Rinse with clean water. Cover with sterile bandage.
    - *Escalate now:* Spurting blood, dizziness, deep/chest/neck/groin wound → **Alert crew & log to Earth**.
  - **Burn:**
    1. Move away from heat. Remove rings or watches before swelling.
    2. Rinse under cool running water for 5–20 minutes. Do not use ice.
    3. Cover loosely with clean, dry non-stick gauze. Do not pop blisters.
    4. Take approved pain reliever if needed.
    - *Escalate now:* Larger than palm, face/hands/airway, white/charred, chemical/electrical → **Alert crew & log to Earth**.
  - **Eye injury / vision change:**
    1. Do not rub or press on your eye.
    2. *Sudden vision loss, bleeding, or stuck object?* Stop. Cover loosely with shield/cup.
    3. *Chemical splash or dust?* Tilt injured eye lower than other eye.
    4. Flush gently with clean water/eyewash for 20 minutes continuously.
    - *Escalate now:* Vision change, stuck object, bleeding, or lasting pain → **Alert crew & log to Earth**.
  - **Allergic reaction:**
    1. Stop contact with trigger if known.
    2. *Breathing trouble, throat tightness, lip/tongue swelling, or dizziness?* Anaphylaxis suspected.
    3. Jab prescribed epinephrine injector firmly into outer thigh. Hold per label.
    4. *Mild skin rash or itch only?* Clean skin; use kit antihistamine/cream.
    - *Escalate now:* Any breathing, swelling, or dizziness symptom → **Alert crew immediately & log to Earth**.
  - *(Optional 5th)* **Dizziness / near-fainting:**
    1. Stop moving. Sit or lie flat right away.
    2. *No chest pain or stroke signs?* Cross legs and tense leg/core muscles.
    3. Check blood pressure once stable. Sip water if alert.
    - *Escalate now:* Not better in 1–2 min, fainted, chest pain, weakness/slurred speech → **Alert crew & log to Earth**.
- **Proposed safety wording for Emergency Mode:**
  - *Header / Pre-step banner:* **"Decision support only — not a medical diagnosis. On Mars, do not wait for Earth if red flags appear."**
  - *Persistent escalation bar:* **"Red flag or getting worse? Tap to alert crewmate/CMO now and queue report for Earth."**

## Not verified / open questions
- **Complete 1–100 IMM ranked table:** NASA does not publish the full 100-condition IMM/iMED input and output table with exact per-condition incidence and pLOCL ranks publicly in a single open document; public sources report aggregate mission metrics (TME, pEVAC, pLOCL, CHI) and selected top drivers (such as Partial Eye Chemical Burn, renal stones, sepsis, cardiac events).
- **Exact ISS Medical Checklist text:** The operational *ISS Medical Checklist* itself is an internal NASA flight-operations document (referenced in NTRS 20110010924 and NASA-STD-3001), so exact NASA crew checklist step wording could not be quoted verbatim and terrestrial steps were grounded in 2024 AHA/ARC, Red Cross, NHS, and NIDDK guidelines.
- **Exact Shuttle ICD-9 event percentages:** Secondary summaries often cite Shuttle STS-1 to STS-89 breakdowns (e.g., 42.2% space adaptation syndrome), but the exact primary Billica (2000) table page could not be directly verified in *Safe Passage* beyond the Ch. 5 behavioral count (34 events / 2.86 per person-year).
- **Onboard Mars medical kit formulary & dose specifics:** Specific analgesic/antihistamine/epinephrine stock quantities and shelf-life stability for a Day-120 Mars transit depend on mission kit design (ExMC MedCap trades) and are not fixed in public standards.
