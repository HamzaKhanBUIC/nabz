# R8: Crewmate collapses in front of Sara

## Short answer
- **First 60 seconds (2024–2025 AHA, ARC & ERC):** Check scene safety (and cabin toxic-air alarms/smoke in space) first, tap shoulders and shout to check responsiveness, open airway (head-tilt, chin-lift if no trauma) and check normal breathing for **≤10 seconds** (treat agonal gasps as cardiac arrest), and **call crewmates/CMO + fetch the AED immediately** if unresponsive.
- **Not breathing normally & AED hardware:** Adult lone-rescuer CPR uses **100–120 compressions/min**, depth **5–6 cm (2–2.4 in)**, and a **30:2** ratio (or continuous compressions if untrained/unable to give breaths); apply an AED as soon as available (**1 shock** if advised, then **immediately resume CPR for 2 min**), but **only shock once the crewmate is restrained on an electrically isolated surface**. The ISS carries a space-modified **LIFEPAK 1000 AED**; for Mars exploration kits, ExMC trades AED mass/volume via **IMPACT** while Mars task lists include AED defibrillation on an electrically isolated crewmate.
- **Microgravity CPR & airway positioning:** Without gravity, compressions push rescuer and patient apart. DGLRM/ESAM guidelines recommend the **Evetts-Russomano (ER)** method for immediate lone-rescuer BLS (no straps needed; simulations measure **105.6/min** rate but shallow **37.4–44.0 mm** depth), then switching to **Handstand (HS)** once restrained on the **Crew Medical Restraint System (CMRS)** (**40.1–49.3 mm** depth; **91.9–98.3/min** rate); **Reverse Bear Hug** (**23.9 mm**) and **Side-Straddle** (**19.8 mm**) are far shallower. A floor recovery position does **not** work in 0g: strap/tether the breathing unconscious crewmate (ideally to CMRS), maintain airway via head-tilt/chin-lift (or jaw thrust if trauma), keep suction ready, and wait for **≥2 crew** before placing a **supraglottic airway (SGA)**.
- **Likely in-flight causes & decisive action:** NASA IMM/HRP track **toxic air** (CO2, NH3, smoke → **check alarms & put on PBA mask first**), **cardiac arrest** (**check breathing ≤10 s → ER CPR + AED**), **orthostatic/vasovagal syncope** (**check pulse/BP & keep flat/restrained**), **hypoglycemia** (**check glucose; nothing by mouth if unresponsive**), **seizure** (**pad from hardware collisions; never restrain limbs or put anything in mouth**), and **head injury** (**stabilize head/neck; check pupils/trauma**).
- **Escalation & Should-version proposal:** Sara must alert/wake crewmates and the CMO **immediately** for any non-response, abnormal breathing, seizure, trauma, or toxic-air alarm, and queue a structured Flight Surgeon report (timeline, cabin gases, AVPU/GCS, vitals/glucose, AED ECG/shocks, interventions). For the *Should* build, we propose **4 crewmate scenarios** under one triage entry: **Unresponsive & NOT breathing**, **Unresponsive & BREATHING**, **Seizure**, and **Toxic air / Smoke collapse**.

## What this means for the app (proposal)
- **Single entry triage ("For a crewmate → Someone collapsed"):** Because Sara may encounter any cause of collapse, start with a 3-tap triage gate matching the first 60 seconds, then route into **4 proposed Should-version scenarios**. Keep a persistent **"Alert crew / CMO"** button on every screen (wakes crewmates immediately; Earth alert queues asynchronously).
- **Pre-step safety banner (proposal):** *"Decision support only — not a medical diagnosis. On Mars, wake crew/CMO immediately; do not wait for Earth."*
- **Initial Triage Screens (first 60 s — max ~12 words/step):**
  1. Check cabin air alarms, smoke, or chemical smell. **Put on mask if unsafe.**
  2. Tap crewmate's shoulders and shout loudly: *"Are you alright?"*
  3. **Tap "Alert Crew / CMO" now** if they do not wake up.
  4. Tilt head, lift chin (jaw thrust if head hit). **Check normal breathing ≤10 s.**
  - *Branch buttons:* **Not breathing / gasping** → Scenario 1 | **Breathing, still out** → Scenario 2 | **Shaking / seizure** → Scenario 3 | **Air alarm / fumes** → Scenario 4.

- **Proposed Scenario 1 — Unresponsive & NOT breathing normally (Cardiac Arrest + AED):**
  1. Shout for crewmates, AED, and medical restraint board (CMRS) immediately.
  2. Float over crewmate; hook and cross ankles behind their shoulders (ER method).
  3. Push center of chest hard and fast: **100–120/min**, full recoil. *(On-screen metronome pulse.)*
  4. If trained and able, give **2 breaths every 30 compressions**. Otherwise keep pushing.
  5. When helper arrives, strap crewmate onto **isolated CMRS** before using AED.
  6. Switch to **Handstand CPR** (feet braced on wall) once strapped in CMRS.
  7. Turn on AED, stick pads on bare chest, follow voice prompts.
  8. **Clear everyone** while AED analyzes or shocks. Resume compressions right away.
  - **Red flags (immediate crew + priority Earth report):** Any cardiac arrest, no return of pulse/breathing, repeated shocks, suspected toxic air or trauma cause.

- **Proposed Scenario 2 — Unresponsive & BREATHING normally (Fainting / Head Injury / Low Sugar check):**
  1. Strap or tether crewmate gently to CMRS or handhold so they cannot drift.
  2. Keep airway open: tilt head, lift chin. *(If head hit, jaw thrust only.)*
  3. Keep suction ready. **Never put food, water, or pills in their mouth.**
  4. Check for **head bump, bleeding, or unequal pupils**; keep head/neck steady.
  5. Measure **HR, BP, SpO2, and fingerstick glucose** once helper or kit arrives.
  6. Stay with them; recheck breathing every minute until fully awake.
  - **Red flags (immediate CMO + Earth report):** Stops breathing or gasping, stays out >1–2 min, head trauma/unequal pupils, seizure, chest pain, or low blood sugar while unable to swallow.

- **Proposed Scenario 3 — Seizure (Active convulsions & post-seizure):**
  1. **Tap "Alert Crew / CMO" now.** Start the on-screen seizure timer.
  2. Push hard/sharp floating gear away. Cushion head and body from wall hits.
  3. **Do NOT hold limbs down. Do NOT put anything in their mouth.**
  4. Once shaking stops, tether gently and check normal breathing for **≤10 seconds**.
  5. If **not breathing normally**, go to **CPR + AED** immediately.
  6. If breathing normally, keep airway open, keep suction ready, monitor until awake.
  - **Red flags (immediate CMO + Earth report):** First-time seizure (always escalate), lasts **>5 min**, back-to-back seizures, breathing trouble/choking, head injury, or not waking **5–10 min** after shaking stops.

- **Proposed Scenario 4 — Toxic air / Smoke collapse (CO2, Ammonia, Fire):**
  1. **Put on your emergency breathing mask (PBA) FIRST** before touching crewmate.
  2. Trigger cabin emergency alarm and alert all crew immediately.
  3. Put mask/oxygen on crewmate and move them to a clean-air module.
  4. Close module hatches/isolate airflow per spacecraft Caution & Warning steps.
  5. If ammonia touched skin or eyes, remove contaminated clothes and flush with water.
  6. Once in clean air, check breathing (**≤10 s**); start CPR if not breathing.
  - **Red flags (immediate CMO + Earth report):** Any unconsciousness after fumes/smoke, hoarse voice, soot around mouth/nose, breathing trouble, chest pain, or gas levels above safe limits.

- **Proposed "Report to Earth" auto-summary template (for Flight Surgeon / BME):**
  - **When & What:** UTC + mission day 120 timestamp, collapse context (witnessed/unwitnessed, activity, trauma/impact, seizure duration).
  - **Cabin air:** CO2, O2, and any NH3/CO/smoke alarm status.
  - **Neuro & Airway:** Responsiveness (AVPU/GCS), pupils, breathing status, airway adjunct used.
  - **Vitals & Labs:** HR, BP, SpO2, temperature, fingerstick blood glucose.
  - **Actions & Hardware:** CPR start/duration & method, AED rhythm/shocks + ECG file, O2/medications given, current crewmate status.

## Not verified / open questions
- **Exact Mars transit medical kit manifest & AED model:** NASA has not published a locked hardware manifest for a future Mars transit spacecraft; public NTRS documents confirm the ISS carries a modified **LIFEPAK 1000** (`20110022379`), while ExMC uses **IMPACT** (`20240011623`) to trade AED inclusion against mass/volume constraints and Mars crew task lists (`20190001401`) include AED use.
- **Exact text of the ISS IMG Medical Checklist (`JSC-48522`):** While referenced by NTRS/NASA documents for ISS cardiac arrest, choking, toxic exposure, and CMO reporting workflows, the operational checklist PDF itself is an internal flight-operations document not publicly hosted in full on NTRS; clinical steps here are grounded in 2024 AHA/ARC, 2025 AHA/ERC, and DGLRM/ESAM microgravity guidelines.
- **Complete 100-condition IMM CliFF incidence table:** Individual baseline incidence rates and severity rankings across all 100 IMM conditions (including exact flight incidence for hypoglycemia, seizure, and major head trauma) are not published in a single open table; public sources confirm these conditions are modeled in IMM/CliFFs alongside aggregate DRM outcomes (`PMC8971481`).
- **Kordi et al. (2011) exact per-method mm/rate table:** PubMed (`PMID: 22195400`) indexes the preliminary comparison of ER, HS, and RBH, but the exact per-method numeric breakdown from the 2011 brief report was behind the journal paywall and not directly readable; full numeric comparisons are therefore sourced from the larger 2014 follow-up by the same group (**Rehnberg et al. 2014**, `PMID: 25022155`) plus **Evetts et al. 2005** (`PMID: 15892553`), **Jay et al. 2003** (`PMID: 14620476`), and **Schmitz et al. 2022** (`PMC9785883`).
