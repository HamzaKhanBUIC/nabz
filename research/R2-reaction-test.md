# R2: reaction test ("tap when green")

## Short answer

- **What NASA uses:** NASA uses the **10-minute Psychomotor Vigilance Test (PVT)** as the laboratory gold standard, the **3-minute brief PVT (PVT-B)** inside the ISS **Reaction Self Test (RST)** (plus a short sleep/mood survey), and a **3-minute PVT** as subtest 10 of the 10-test **NASA Cognition** battery (which probes sensorimotor speed, visual learning, working memory, abstraction, spatial orientation, emotion recognition, reasoning, processing speed, risk-taking, and vigilant attention).
- **Best metrics & definitions:** Use **mean reciprocal reaction time (`1/RT`, response speed in `s^-1`)** and **lapses** as primary outcomes, avoiding raw **mean/median RT** because extreme slow responses skew distributions and slash sleep-loss sensitivity. Standard **10-min PVT** defines valid responses as `RT >= 100 ms`, **false starts** as responses before stimulus or `RT < 100 ms`, and **lapses** as `RT >= 500 ms`; **3-min PVT-B** lowers the lapse cutoff to **`>= 355 ms`** (roughly cutting sensitivity loss) and also computes a 0–100% **Performance Score**.
- **Normal values & day-to-day spread:** Well-rested healthy adults on PVT-10 show median response speed around `3.88–4.03 s^-1` (~248–258 ms) and `0` median lapses (IQR `1`), and on PVT-3 around `4.12–4.31 s^-1` (~232–243 ms) with `1` median lapse (IQR `3`). ISS astronauts pre-flight average a Cognition PVT Speed (`10 - 1/RT`) of `5.25 ± 0.24` (`1/RT ≈ 4.75 s^-1`, accuracy `0.97 ± 0.04`) and RST Overall Performance Score `0.95 ± 0.02`; in-flight ISS RST Likelihood of Slowest 50% (LRM-50) averages `-33.0 ± 12.7` across days, while Belgrano II Antarctic crew averaged `282–305 ms` morning/evening with `83–130 ms` within-session RT SD.
- **How sleep loss, workload, and spaceflight change results:** Acute 33 h total sleep deprivation produces very large effects on 10-min PVT (`ES = 1.93` for `1/RT`, `1.86` for lapses), and 5 nights of 4 h sleep restriction yields `ES = 1.21` (`1/RT`) and `0.91` (lapses); in the Cognition battery after 1 night without sleep, PVT shows the largest decline (`d = 1.00`), ahead of DSST (`d = 0.68`) and Matrix Reasoning (`d = 0.65`). On the ISS, sleeping `<= 6 h` (34.9% of nights) significantly slows PVT-B speed (`p < 0.0001`), high workload shortens sleep, and early ISS flight slows Cognition PVT speed by `Δz = 0.50` (`β = 0.62`, `20.2%` of flight/post-flight observations `<= -1.5 SD`).
- **Personal baseline & meaningful change:** Unlike complex cognitive tests (9 of 10 Cognition subtests show protracted learning across 15 sessions), **PVT mean `1/RT`, lapses, and mean/median RT have virtually zero practice effects across 16+ administrations**, so 1 familiarization session plus a few resting sessions establishes a stable personal baseline. Meaningful impairment in NASA studies is operationalized as `z <= -1.5 SD` below baseline (Cognition) or shifts exceeding `±0.1 s^-1` in `1/RT` / `±1` lapse (PVT calibration threshold).
- **Web/tablet browser limits:** JavaScript's `performance.now()` offers `100 µs` (`0.1 ms`) precision (`5 µs` if cross-origin isolated), so timer resolution is not the bottleneck—**OS, browser compositor, display refresh, and touchscreen pipelines add ~15–87 ms mean lag and ~8–28 ms jitter (SD)**, exceeding Basner & Dinges' precision target (`bias <= ±5 ms`, `SD <= 10 ms`). Therefore, never compare raw ms against desktop/hardware norms or swap posture/devices: **lock Sara to the same device, browser, and hand/orientation, calibrate baseline on that exact setup, use `1/RT` and relative z-scores, and keep `>= 355 ms` lapse comparisons strictly within-subject**.

## What this means for the app (proposal)

These design recommendations are **proposals** grounded in the findings above (not NASA prescriptions):

1. **Test protocol (3-min PVT-B model, offline-first):**
   - Implement a **3-minute visual stimulus test** ("tap when target/green signal appears") with **2–5 s random inter-stimulus interval** (matching ISS RST), **1 s RT feedback** shown after each valid tap (part of the next ISI), `RT < 100 ms` or pre-stimulus taps flagged as **False Start (`FS`)**, and `RT >= 30,000 ms` timeout logged as a **Lapse + alert**.
   - Keep the 3-minute duration fixed across all days—never mix 3-min and 10-min sessions—and pair the test with a **15-second pre-test check** (prior-night sleep hours + 1–10 subjective fatigue/stress/workload rating, mirroring RST) so Sara sees *why* speed dropped.
   - Respect project UI accessibility rules: do **not** signal stimulus or status by color alone (combine color change with a clear geometric/text cue such as a bold millisecond counter appearing inside the target box, high-contrast dark UI, `>= 12 pt` text).

2. **Primary metrics to compute and store locally:**
   - **Primary alertness metric:** **`mean 1/RT` (response speed in `s^-1`, i.e., `mean(1000 / RT_ms)`)** and **`slowest 10% 1/RT`**. Do **not** use raw mean RT for alerts (keep median RT only as a secondary human-readable number in ms alongside `1/RT`).
   - **Secondary operational metrics:** **PVT-B Lapses (`RT >= 355 ms`)**, **False Starts**, and **Performance Score (`100% * (1 - (lapses + false_starts) / (valid_stimuli + false_starts))`)**.

3. **Setting Sara's personal baseline (Day 120 realistic state):**
   - Because PVT has **no material practice effect**, discard only **1 brief familiarization run** (or first 3–5 practice trials), then compute Sara's rolling **personal baseline (`mean`, `SD`)** from her **first 5–7 rested, normal-duty daily check-ins** (or a rolling 14-day well-rested window anchored to days reporting `>= 7 h` sleep, noting Day 120 astronauts already have chronic flight adaptation).
   - **Meaningful-change alert tiers (relative to her own baseline, never absolute ms cutoffs):**
     - **Normal:** within `-1.0 SD` of her baseline `mean 1/RT` (or `<= +1` lapse vs her median).
     - **Heads-up / Monitor:** `-1.0` to `-1.5 SD` slower in `1/RT`, or a `>= 0.1 s^-1` drop (exceeding the `PMC8240655` response-speed tolerance) paired with `< 6 h` sleep.
     - **Slower than normal (Actionable Check-in):** **`z <= -1.5 SD`** below her personal `mean 1/RT` (matching the Cognition `<= -1.5 SD` low-score criterion) or a sharp spike in `>= 355 ms` lapses / false starts — framed strictly as decision support (e.g., deferring non-urgent precision tasks, double-checking procedures, prioritizing recovery sleep), never diagnosis.

4. **Handling browser & tablet timing lag in code:**
   - Timestamp stimulus presentation inside `requestAnimationFrame` (`rAF` callback `DOMHighResTimeStamp`, or `performance.now()` immediately after frame paint) rather than `setTimeout`, and read `event.timeStamp` on `pointerdown` (not `click`, which fires only after tap/press release). Keep the event timestamp and `rAF` clock in the same `performance.now()` timebase (and verify `event.timeStamp` matches `performance.now()` monotonicity on the target browser).
   - **Hardware/posture lock:** Require Sara to use the **same tablet/laptop, same browser, and same hand posture** (e.g., always landscape thumb OR always tabletop index finger, because posture alone shifts mean RT by `~20 ms`) and disable battery-saver throttling during the 3-minute test.
   - Because uncalibrated browser + touchscreen latency adds `~15–87 ms` bias and `8–28 ms` SD, **suppress hardware-norm ms labels** ("normal is 220 ms") in the UI or explicitly benchmark device bias if ever displaying absolute ms; drive all traffic-light/alert logic purely from **within-person `z-scores` and `Δ(1/RT)`**.

## Not verified / open questions

- **Within-person day-to-day SD on a well-rested 3-min browser/tablet PVT-B:** The ISS Cognition study (`PMC11614644`) reports between-astronaut pre-flight SD (`0.24` on `10 - 1/RT`), and `PMC9246897` reports pooled LRM-50 SD (`12.7`), while `PMC6659627` reports within-session trial RT SD (`83–130 ms`), but exact day-to-day *intra-individual* SD for a single rested astronaut taking daily 3-min PVT-B tests over 120+ days was not extracted directly from ISS raw datasets in the accessed papers.
- **Tablet Safari/Chrome mobile-browser touch jitter under modern 120 Hz ProMotion / Android displays:** `PMID 28095256` measured 5th-gen iPod touch latency (`68.53 ms` mean, high inter-individual variance), and `PMC8367876` / `PMC7512138` benchmarked desktop browsers (`Win10/macOS`); quantitative hardware-photodiode latency & SD distributions specifically for 2024–2026 iPadOS Safari and Android tablet Chrome `pointerdown` vs `rAF` were not retrieved here.
- **Isolating microgravity/radiation vs sleep loss on Mars transit Day 120:** ISS studies (`PMC8919197`, `PMC11614644`) and Mars-520d (`PMC3968121`) strongly implicate sleep duration `<= 6 h` and mission phase/workload transitions in PVT slowing rather than confinement alone (`PMC7198903`), while `HDBR ± CO2` (`PMC8262780`) showed modest `-0.25 to -0.37 SD` slowing. Whether deep-space transit beyond LEO (no Earth resupply, 40-min comms lag, GCR exposure) creates an extra chronic PVT drift beyond sleep restriction remains unverified in human missions.
- **Exact canonical `nasa.gov` Experiment Explorer URL slugs:** Specific `nasa.gov` Life Sciences Data Archive / Station Research Explorer URL IDs for RST and Cognition were not fetched directly because peer-reviewed open-access PMC full texts (`PMC3197786`, `PMC4691281`, `PMC8919197`, `PMC9246897`, `PMC11614644`) provided primary protocol details and statistics.
