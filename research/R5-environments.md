# R5: Station / Moon / Mars environment switch

## Short answer
- **Communication delays & gaps scale from real-time to full blackouts.** ISS runs with `< 1 s` end-to-end TDRS delay and near-continuous coverage after the Guam terminal closed the Indian Ocean Zone of Exclusion (with VHF voice backup). The Moon (Artemis) has `~1.3 s` one-way / `~2.6 s` round-trip speed-of-light delay (4–12 s one-way operational latency tested in NASA studies) plus direct-to-Earth gaps at the lunar South Pole and far side until LCRNS relays deploy. Mars ranges from `~3 min` (or `0 min` at Earth departure) to `~22.4 min` one-way (`~6–44.8 min` round-trip), loses direct surface line-of-sight `~12 h/sol` from planetary rotation, and hits a `~2–3 week` solar conjunction disruption/command moratorium every `~26 months`.
- **Medical support shifts along NASA's EIMO continuum from ground-directed to crew-autonomous.** Under NASA's Earth-Independent Medical Operations (EIMO) framework and historical NASA-STD-3001 Levels of Care (I–V), ISS is Earth-reliant (ground Flight Surgeon directs a 40-hour-trained CMO; evacuation in `< 24 h`). The Moon is transitional (Level III–IV; near-real-time oversight, 3–6 day abort return). Mars requires Level V / Relative-to-Total Autonomy (physician CMO required, redundant trained crew, onboard CDSS/AI assistants, no evacuation, no resupply).
- **Escalation rules change by setting.** On ISS: **call the flight surgeon now** via real-time audio/video or Private Medical Conference (after immediate life-saving first aid). On the Moon: **hybrid** — call now when in coverage for non-emergent findings, **act then report** during South Pole/far-side coverage gaps or acute emergencies (multi-day return precludes waiting for rescue). On Mars: **act then report** via asynchronous store-and-forward messaging for acute/worrying findings, and **decide alone and report later** during solar conjunction blackouts (`~2–3 weeks`) or `~12 h/sol` unlinked windows.
- **Health check-ins also differ on evacuation, gravity, radiation, circadian rhythm, and dust.** Evacuation goes from `1.5–36 h` (`≤ 24 h` requirement) on ISS, to `72–144 h` (3–6 days) from the Moon, to impossible on Mars (650–1,250-day missions). Surface gravity is `~0 g` (ISS), `1/6 g` (Moon), and `0.38 g` / `3/8 g` (Mars, after `~0 g` transit). Radiation stays well under NASA's `600 mSv` career limit on ISS (`40–90 mSv`/mission) and short Artemis flights (`< 20–30 mSv` GCR), approaches `~350 mSv` for 1-year lunar stays, and hits `~525–1,650 mSv` (`~1,000–1,200 mSv` typical) on Mars missions. Surface settings also add toxic dust (`0.3 mg/m³` lunar PEL; `0.1 mg/m³` proposed 30-day Mars PEL with perchlorates) and Mars's `24.6 h` (`~24 h 39 m`) circadian sol.
- **Smallest valid settings switch (proposal).** A 3-mode environment profile (**Station / LEO**, **Moon / Cislunar**, **Mars / Deep Space**) changing only four parameters — *link & delay badge*, *escalation CTA*, *evacuation/support status*, and *active environment context notes* (gravity, radiation, dust/sol) — captures the real NASA operational differences without multiplying UI complexity.

## What this means for the app (proposal)
*All items below are design proposals for the team to decide on, not locked product decisions.*

- **Decision recommendation for the team:** **Build a lightweight 3-setting environment switch** (*ISS / LEO Station*, *Moon / Artemis Base*, *Mars Transit & Surface*). It directly answers the challenge prompt ("adapt to different settings"), makes the Mars day-120 persona contrast visually obvious to judges in 1 click, and requires almost zero extra backend logic if scoped strictly to the four parameters below.
- **Smallest accurate settings switch (4-parameter config object):**
  1. `comm_delay_label` & `link_status`:
     - **ISS / LEO:** `< 1 s delay · Real-time link (TDRS)`
     - **Moon / Artemis:** `~2.6 s round-trip (4–12 s ops lag) · Terrain/far-side gaps`
     - **Mars (Transit / Surface):** `6–45 min round-trip (Day 120 demo: e.g. 20 min OW / 40 min RT) · ~12 h/sol or 2–3 wk solar blackout mode`
  2. `escalation_mode` (drives the Action Card primary CTA):
     - **ISS / LEO:** **Call Flight Surgeon now** (real-time MCC consult; immediate first aid if acute).
     - **Moon / Artemis:** **Call Flight Surgeon (if linked) · Act then report if unlinked/acute** (stabilize onboard; 3–6 day return).
     - **Mars:** **Act now, log & send async to Earth** (or **Decide alone & queue for post-blackout sync** when solar conjunction toggle is on).
  3. `evacuation_and_support`:
     - **ISS / LEO:** `Medevac ≤ 24 h · Ground-directed care`
     - **Moon / Artemis:** `Return 3–6 days · Hybrid crew/ground`
     - **Mars:** `No evacuation · Earth-independent (Level V)`
  4. `environment_context` (one-line clinical banner on check-in / action card, not separate complex algorithms):
     - **ISS / LEO:** `~0 g · 24 h Earth clock · 40–90 mSv/mission`
     - **Moon / Artemis:** `1/6 g · Surface EVA fitness + lunar dust caution`
     - **Mars:** `0 g transit → 0.38 g surface · 24 h 39 m sol · Elevated radiation (~1,000–1,200 mSv) · Perchlorate dust`
- **Keep core thresholds unchanged across modes:** Do **not** alter personal-normal baseline math or emergency first-aid steps per environment for the Saturday MVP (evidence on partial-gravity physiological norms and Mars CNS radiation drift is still incomplete per NASA). Only change **communication latency, escalation CTA, evacuation context, and environmental reminders**.
- **Optional demo toggle inside Mars mode:** Add a single `"Solar conjunction blackout"` switch that shows offline queue / `"Decide alone and report later"` mode cleanly, reinforcing the offline-first design.

## Not verified / open questions
- **Current routine ISS LOS duration:** While NASA confirms GRGT closed the historic 15% Indian Ocean Zone of Exclusion for near-continuous TDRS coverage, exact current minutes-per-orbit of brief attitude/structural Ku-band dropouts on ISS were not found in the primary NASA pages reviewed.
- **Formal numbered EIMO "autonomy levels" table:** `20260001797` defines a Relative Heteronomy → Total Autonomy spectrum with four CMO participation bands, and `20170000358` defines historical Levels of Care I–V, while `NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 1 Rev C` replaces LoC tables with probabilistic risk tailoring. NASA has not published a single agency-wide "EIMO Level 1–5" standard table analogous to Levels of Care I–V in the public NTRS files reviewed (full activity diagrams inside the `20240001402` MBSE report are access-controlled via NASA NAMS).
- **Exact human Mars conjunction protocol:** The `~2 week` (`13–21 day`) conjunction command moratorium is documented for robotic Mars missions and modeled geometrically for 2039 crewed trajectories; NASA's operational crewed flight rules (e.g., whether solar-L4 relays will eliminate the blackout) remain undecided.
- **Mars dust PEL status and partial-gravity physiological curves:** The Mars dust `0.1 mg/m³` 30-day continuous PEL in `NASA/SP-20260005907` is explicitly a **proposed** standard pending authentic sample return, and human physiological adaptation curves at `1/6 g` and `0.38 g` (beyond Apollo short stays and terrestrial analogs) are active NASA HRP research gaps rather than settled clinical thresholds.
- **Brief wording note ("1/3 g" vs `0.38 g`):** The brief mentions Mars `1/3 g`; NASA technical sources consistently specify **`0.38 g` / `3/8 g`** (`37.8%` of Earth gravity), so the table uses `0.38 g` (`~3/8 g`).
