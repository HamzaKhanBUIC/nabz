# R3: Guided blood pressure measurement

## Short answer

- **Self-measurement steps (one short instruction per screen):**
  1. Skip caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, and exercise for 30 minutes beforehand.
  2. Empty your bladder before starting.
  3. Fit the right-sized cuff onto bare skin just above your elbow bend.
  4. Anchor your feet or body so you stay relaxed and still.
  5. Sit or float quietly for 5 minutes with no talking or screens.
  6. Rest your bare arm relaxed at heart level.
  7. Stay silent and still while the cuff takes Reading 1.
  8. Wait 1 minute quietly, then take Reading 2 and log both.
- **Current categories (AHA/ACC 2025 & ESC 2024):** Under the 2025 AHA/ACC Guideline, adult categories are **Normal** (<120 and <80 mm Hg), **Elevated** (120–129 and <80 mm Hg), **Stage 1 Hypertension** (130–139 or 80–89 mm Hg), **Stage 2 Hypertension** (≥140 or ≥90 mm Hg), and **Severe Hypertension** (>180 and/or >120 mm Hg; also called **Hypertensive Crisis** in AHA patient guidance). Under the 2024 ESC Guideline, categories simplify to **Non-elevated** (<120/<70 mm Hg), **Elevated** (120–139/70–89 mm Hg office; 120–134/70–84 mm Hg home), and **Hypertension** (≥140/≥90 mm Hg office; ≥135/≥85 mm Hg home).
- **Red flags (exact thresholds):** **>180/120 mm Hg** (AHA/ACC 2025; wait at least 1 minute and retest): if paired with acute organ-damage symptoms (chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness/weakness, vision changes, difficulty speaking), treat as a **Hypertensive Emergency** requiring immediate urgent action; if persistent >180/120 mm Hg *without* symptoms, classify as **Severe Hypertension** requiring prompt medical contact/review. **<90/60 mm Hg** (NHLBI) or a drop of **≥20 mm Hg systolic / ≥10 mm Hg diastolic** on standing (if in gravity) is a red flag if accompanied by dizziness, fainting, confusion, cold/clammy/pale skin, rapid shallow breathing, or rapid weak pulse (shock/hypoperfusion).
- **In space & months 3–6 (Sara at Day 120):** Eliminating Earth's hydrostatic gradient causes a rapid ~1.5–2.0 L headward fluid shift and a ~10%–15% plasma volume contraction. Across **months 3–6 on the ISS** (85–192 days), 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure drops below pre-flight upright/seated averages (**−8 to −14 mm Hg systolic**, **−9 mm Hg diastolic** in Norsk et al. 2015; unchanged diastolic in Fu et al. 2019), while **heart rate stays unchanged** and in-flight BP/HR closely match pre-flight **supine** baselines. Measuring in 0g removes vertical hydrostatic errors, so posture shifts from "feet flat on floor" to **anchoring the body with foot loops/restraints** to avoid floating muscle tension, using an **automated oscillometric cuff** (not stethoscope auscultation) with the arm relaxed near heart level.
- **NASA ISS practice:** Operational monitoring under the **Crew Health Care System (CHeCS) Health Maintenance System (HMS)** uses the **BP/ECG** assembly (combining a **SunTech Tango** oscillometric Stress BP monitor—manual or 1–9 min auto intervals—with ECG; **Philips Tempus Pro** has also been evaluated for exploration telemedicine), while research uses **Mobil-O-Graph** (24-h/13-h ABPM) and **Portapres** (continuous finger cuff). Schedule includes **Periodic Fitness Evaluations (PFE)** on the CEVIS cycle ergometer (baseline around FD14, then **every 30 days**), **Periodic Health Status (PHS)** vital checks by the Crew Medical Officer, weekly/regular **Private Medical Conferences (PMCs)**, and ad-hoc flight surgeon checks.

## What this means for the app (proposal)

- **Proposed guided measurement flow (8 short screens, 1 instruction per screen):**
  1. *Prep check:* "No caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, or exercise in the last 30 min?" (Yes / Skip with flag)
  2. *Bladder:* "Empty your bladder before starting."
  3. *Cuff fit:* "Wrap the matched cuff snugly on bare skin just above your elbow bend."
  4. *Anchor:* "Lock your feet or body restraint so you can relax without drifting."
  5. *Rest timer (5:00 countdown):* "Rest quietly for 5 minutes. No talking or screen tasks."
  6. *Arm position:* "Rest your arm relaxed at heart level (mid-chest)."
  7. *Reading 1:* "Stay silent and still while the cuff inflates."
  8. *1-minute gap + Reading 2:* "Wait 1 minute quietly, then take Reading 2." (Auto-average both readings and save.)
- **Proposed Mars-transit baseline adjustment ("personal normal zone"):**
  - Compare Sara's Day-120 BP against her **pre-flight supine** baseline (or her established in-flight steady-state baseline from months 1–3), **not** her pre-flight seated/standing baseline—otherwise a normal in-flight SBP drop of 8–14 mm Hg (e.g., 120 → 106 mm Hg) will look falsely low.
  - Because **heart rate remains near pre-flight supine baseline**, a rising resting HR paired with falling BP (<90/60 mm Hg or a sharp drop from her personal normal zone) is a stronger early warning of dehydration, illness, or bleeding than low SBP alone.
- **Proposed offline red-flag escalation logic (40-min Earth delay safe):**
  - **If >180/120 mm Hg:** Prompt a 1-minute quiet retest. Ask symptom checklist (chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness/weakness, vision change, trouble speaking). If *any* symptom = trigger **Emergency Mode** immediately without waiting for Earth. If *no* symptoms and still >180/120 = flag **Severe Hypertension**, advise urgent flight-surgeon contact and repeat monitoring.
  - **If <90/60 mm Hg (or >15–20 mm Hg drop from Sara's in-flight normal):** Ask hypoperfusion symptoms (dizziness, fainting/near-fainting, confusion, cold/clammy skin, rapid shallow breathing). If *any* present = trigger **Emergency Mode** (shock/hypotension protocol).
  - **If ≥130/80 mm Hg (AHA Stage 1+) or ≥135/85 mm Hg (ESC home HTN) persistently across 3–7 days:** Surface an **Action Card** flagging sustained elevation above her in-flight normal zone and prompting review at her next PMC.
- **Proposed metadata tag on every reading:** Log `cuff_size`, `restraint_used`, `prep_flags` (caffeine/exercise/bladder), and `reading_1` + `reading_2` separately so noisy single readings (e.g., arm tension while floating) don't pollute her personal normal zone trend.
