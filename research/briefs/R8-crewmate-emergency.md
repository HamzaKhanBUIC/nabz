# Brief R8: crewmate collapses in front of Sara

First read `research/briefs/00-RULES.md` and follow it.
**Your output file:** `research/R8-crewmate-emergency.md`. Write nothing else.

## Goal

Emergency mode will get a second button: **For a crewmate**. Sara is mission commander on day 120 of a Mars transit, in microgravity. A crewmate suddenly collapses or becomes unresponsive in front of her. Give us correct, sourced steps she can follow on screen, one short instruction per screen. She may be the only one there at first.

R4 already covered emergencies Sara handles on herself (`research/R4-emergencies.md`). Don't repeat those; this brief is about helping someone else.

## Questions

1. **First 60 seconds.** What should one person do first when someone collapses? Use the 2024 AHA / American Red Cross Guidelines for First Aid, current AHA resuscitation guidance and ERC guidelines. Cover:
   - checking for danger
   - checking whether they respond
   - checking breathing, and for how long
   - calling for help
2. **Not breathing normally.**
   - What is the CPR sequence for a lone rescuer? Give compression rate, depth and ratio.
   - When is an AED used, and how?
   - Does the ISS or the planned exploration medical kit carry an AED or defibrillator?
3. **CPR in microgravity.** Which methods has NASA or space-medicine research tested, and how well do they work? For example:
   - the Evetts-Russomano (ER) method
   - the handstand method
   - reverse bear hug
   - side-straddle
   - restraining the patient on the crew medical restraint system
   Which one can one untrained-in-space person do? Cite the studies and their measured compression depth and rate.
4. **Breathing but unresponsive.** What replaces the recovery position when there is no floor? How do you keep the airway open and the person secured?
5. **Likely causes in flight.** Which causes of sudden collapse matter most on a long mission? For example: cardiac arrest, fainting / orthostatic intolerance, low blood sugar, seizure, head injury, toxic air (CO2, ammonia, smoke). Use the NASA Integrated Medical Model and HRP sources where possible.
   - What is the one check or action for each that changes what Sara does?
   - Example: toxic air means put on a mask first.
6. **Seizure.** First aid for a crewmate having a seizure in microgravity.
7. **Escalation.** At which point must Sara wake or call other crew, and what goes in the report to Earth?
8. **Recommendation.** Which 3–4 crewmate scenarios should the Should version include, with screen-by-screen steps (max ~12 words per step) and the red flags for each?
